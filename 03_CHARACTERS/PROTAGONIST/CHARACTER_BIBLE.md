# Protagonist Character Bible

## Canonical identity

A believable 27-year-old Indian man from an urban middle-class family. He is a capable computer engineer whose current emotional state is exhausted, withdrawn and disoriented. He must feel like an ordinary real person, not a fashion model, action hero or generic stock-photo subject.

**Locked appearance**
- Age: approximately 27.
- Skin: medium Indian brown tone with natural variation and realistic texture.
- Face: oval overall shape, naturally proportioned features; avoid an exaggerated jawline or overly sharp cheekbones.
- Eyes: dark brown, natural size and spacing.
- Hair: short, black and slightly messy; mostly hidden whenever the hood is up.
- Facial hair: subtle, light stubble, not a full beard.
- Build: average, neither muscular nor unusually thin.
- Height: approximately 5'9" is a loose guide only, not a locked measurement.
- Overall presentation: understated, believable, slightly tired; no glamour retouching.

Do not invent distinctive scars, tattoos, spectacles, piercings or other identifying features unless later approved. Keep facial proportions, skin tone, eye shape, hairline and stubble stable across all generations.

## Wardrobe — approved

One garment throughout the adult present-day story: plain charcoal / washed-black cotton pullover hoodie.
- Pullover construction; no zipper.
- No logo, text, print, graphic or conspicuous branding.
- Relaxed everyday fit, not oversized.
- Matte cotton/fleece texture with natural folds.
- Keep hood construction, seams, cuffs, hem and drawstrings consistent. If the generator cannot render drawstrings reliably, omit them consistently rather than changing their design between shots.
- Hood UP in bathroom, faceless and present-day station scenes.
- Hood DOWN in the mother-lap ending, naturally gathered behind neck and shoulders. It is the same hoodie, not a different top.

Do not introduce a jacket, second hoodie, shirt-color change or accessories that obscure this continuity.

## Performance and emotional range

The character internalizes emotion. His arc is:
1. Fatigue and confusion.
2. Shame, disconnection and emotional numbness.
3. Attempts to regain control that do not work.
4. Vulnerability and exhaustion in his mother's presence.
5. Quiet relief and a small willingness to begin again.

Use micro-expressions, lowered gaze, held breath, stillness and small posture changes. Avoid theatrical sobbing, exaggerated grimaces, heroic poses, sudden confidence or a broad triumphant smile.

## Backstory — visual evidence only

- Skilled computer engineer.
- Won a solo hackathon.
- Faced several unsuccessful job interviews.
- Was selected for a job but declined joining because of parents' health issues.
- Received admission to a top university but could not attend after an education-loan rejection.
- Experienced a breakup with an office colleague and left a settled job.

Present these as concise memory fragments and carefully designed inserts. Do not add dialogue, explanatory title cards or extra backstory that changes these facts.

## Faceless state

Facelessness is a psychological metaphor, not a literal creature or body transformation. Preserve head size, skull shape, hood, body and clothing. Achieve the effect through controlled shadow, reflection, framing, compositing or a clean editorial cut. Never turn the hood into a black hole or create gore, melted skin or a distorted skull.

## Continuity checklist

- Same apparent age, facial proportions, skin tone, eye color, hairline and light stubble.
- Same average build and understated posture.
- Same charcoal pullover hoodie, material and fit.
- Hood state matches the scene.
- No unapproved accessories or wardrobe changes.
- Emotion stays restrained and physically believable.
