# Master Story Structure

This is the approved high-level structure. Individual shot design remains in development.

## 1. The Disappearance — Intro
Bathroom mirror and washbasin. Hooded protagonist confronts his reflection; water splash and reflection edits lead to a faceless state. The disappearance is psychological and achieved through cuts/compositing, not a magical morph.

## 2. The World Moves On — Verse 1 / Bridge 1
The protagonist stands motionless at a crowded railway station or similar transit space. Crowd movement becomes a time-lapse around him. Diary and personal objects introduce memories.

## 3. Everything He Lost — Verse 1 / Verse 2 memory fragments
- Solo hackathon achievement.
- Failed interviews.
- Selected for a job but declined joining due to parents' health.
- Top-university admission but education-loan rejection.
- Breakup with office colleague; departure from settled job.
Keep each memory brief and visually distinct.

## 4. Losing Himself — Verse 2 / First Chorus
Diary pages, old dreams, achievement objects and the station motif. First chorus is the emotional breakdown: fragments of rejection, empty spaces, phone, laptop and isolation converge.

## 5. Trying Not to Break — Bridge
He tries to hold himself together. His mother quietly notices. He rests his head in her lap. Performance stays restrained.

## 6. The Person She Remembers — Final Chorus
Mother closes her eyes; cut to an actual newborn memory in the same lap. Silent blessing. Return to present with her eyes still closed; one tear falls on his exposed hairline. Face returns through a motivated edit. Son opens his eyes peacefully. End with an intimate, quiet pullback.

## Emotional thesis
He has not got his old life back. He has found the strength to begin living again.

## Contrast rule
The first and final choruses repeat the same lyrics but must not repeat the same visual meaning: first chorus is collapse; final chorus is remembrance, connection and quiet renewal.
