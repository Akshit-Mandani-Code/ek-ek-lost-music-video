# Story Synopsis

A young computer engineer stands before a bathroom mirror, exhausted and unable to recognize the person looking back. As water splashes across his face, his reflection becomes indistinct and finally faceless. The loss is psychological, not a supernatural transformation.

The world continues around him. At a crowded railway station, he remains motionless while commuters blur past. Objects in his room—a diary, laptop, phone, offer letter, medical papers, university correspondence and old achievement—trigger brief memories of the opportunities and relationships he has lost.

The memories reveal a capable young man who won a solo hackathon, faced repeated interview rejections, declined a job opportunity because of his parents' health, lost a university opportunity after an education-loan rejection, and endured a breakup and departure from a settled job. These events appear as concise visual fragments, not long explanatory scenes.

As he struggles to hold himself together, his mother quietly notices. He rests with his head in her lap. She closes her eyes and remembers holding him as a newborn in that same lap. In the memory, her lips move in a silent blessing.

We return to the present with her eyes still closed. A single tear falls onto his exposed hairline. His face returns—not as a magical effect, but through a carefully motivated cinematic transition. He slowly opens his eyes, calmer. He has not recovered his old life; he has found the strength to begin living again.
